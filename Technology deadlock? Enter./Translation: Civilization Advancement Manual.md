## I. Facing Reality: What Exactly Is Today's "Human Science"?

To break the deadlock, we must first see clearly what tool we are holding.

Modern science, especially the mainstream formed over the past few centuries, is essentially an algorithm of **"reductionism + empiricism."** It has two characteristics that must be seen clearly:

**1. Addicted to "correlation," lacking "causal thinking"**

Today's research and engineering rely extremely heavily on big data and probability. The medical field discovers that "people who take a certain drug have a 5% lower mortality rate"; chip engineers find a "barely working" parameter by running millions of simulations. This is called **data fitting.** Everyone only looks at "whether phenomenon A matches phenomenon B," and very few ask: **Does the underlying physical causal chain actually connect?**

When encountering a true physical limit—the second law of thermodynamics, quantum tunneling leakage—systems pieced together by probability instantly collapse into deadlock. Because probability fitting can only interpolate within the parameter space; it cannot cross a physical boundary.

**2. Addicted to "local optimum," lacking "systems thinking"**

Modern science chops knowledge into pieces. Materials people only understand atoms, algorithm people only understand code, hardware people only understand circuits. This is like the blind men touching the elephant: each person's part is correct, but put together it is a grotesque chimera. A chip on which a big company spent tens of billions in R&D ultimately becomes scrap metal because of the physical deadlock of heat dissipation and materials. Because no single person can use **systems theory** to calculate clearly the flow of energy, information, and matter as a whole.

**This is the current "cognitive deadlock" of human thinking: we are using extremely precise methods to walk toward a physically doomed corner.**

### Step Two: Establish True "Causal Law and Systems Thinking"

Look for the **"deadlock points"** and **"leverage solutions"** in the system. Find the physical origin where, once broken, the entire system runs smoothly.


## IV. One Criterion: Three-State Verdict

After mastering causal-law and systems thinking, when facing any proposition, you need to do only one thing: **classify it into one of three states.**

**State One · Proposition Holds**: Under the given explicit assumptions and extreme working conditions, the causal chain is reproducible, and parameters can be locked or verified.

**State Two · Candidate Conjecture, Pending Verification**: Physical axioms permit a solution to exist, but the current derivation has not completed extreme-condition verification, or parameters have not yet been located. This is not "feasible"; this is "pending verification."

**State Three · Physically Absolutely Unsolvable**: Under the current set of axioms, materials, and energy constraints, no solution exists. The proposition is immediately void; project initiation is forbidden; testing is forbidden.

There is no fourth state among these three. There is no ambiguous zone of "insufficient conditions"—insufficient conditions are a subclass of State Two; specify which parameter is missing and by what means it must be locked. Nor is there a vague space of "let's try again"—physically unsolvable means unsolvable; no amount of money, people, or time will yield a feasible solution.

**The three-state verdict is the scale on this ruler.** Master the three states, and you master the measure for distinguishing "truth" from "rhetoric."

**One property that must be stated clearly**: The three-state verdict measures **propositions**, not **people**. A proposition being classified into State Three does not mean the person who proposed it is incompetent. It only means that, under the given axioms and constraints, this causal chain does not connect. Switch to another chain, and the same person may be walking in State One. **Binding the verdict of a proposition to the person is the deepest misuse of this ruler.**

Someone will ask: one replay may be inaccurate; if we do ten, a hundred, a thousand supercomputer runs, will it become statistically significant?

There are only two legitimate uses at the thousand-run scale:

**Option A | Parameter sensitivity scanning.** For the missing parameters of "pending verification" propositions in the simulated track, perform Monte Carlo sampling within their uncertainty range. Run the three-state verdict once for each sampled combination. Statistic: in what proportion of parameter combinations does the proposition fall into State Three. If >95%, the verdict is robust to that parameter uncertainty.

**Option B | Cross-case parallelism.** Not a single case only. Simultaneously select multiple cases with clear timelines and public data, and independently conduct dual-track replay for each. Statistic: the proportion of cases in which the manual issued the correct domain classification in advance. This is **cross-case statistical significance**, which has more physical meaning than a thousand repetitions of a single case.

**Computational load statement**: Option A's single-case parameter scan can be completed on a conventional workstation. Option B grows linearly with the number of cases. **A thousand supercomputer runs are not a necessary item on this proposition.** If one insists on the thousand-run scale, it should be directed toward expanding the case library and blind-test design in Option B, not toward repetition of a single case.

**Blind-test requirement**: The deriver does not know which segment of real history the case under review corresponds to. This eliminates hindsight attribution bias. This is the gold standard for methodological verification.
