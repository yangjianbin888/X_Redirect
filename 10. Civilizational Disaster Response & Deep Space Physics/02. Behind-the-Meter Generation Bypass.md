**02. Behind-the-Meter Generation Bypass: The Global Turbine Hot-Section Casting Deadlock**

**Part 1: Current State** (as of November 2026)

xAI has deployed behind-the-meter (BTM) generation at the Colossus 2 site in Memphis, bypassing the Tennessee Valley Authority grid interconnect queue. As of November 2026, xAI has installed approximately 57 portable gas turbines at a former Duke Energy site in Southaven, Mississippi, just across the state line from Memphis, with capacity scaling toward 1.2 GW under a permanent PSD permit . OpenAI’s Stargate site in Abilene, Texas has similarly deployed gas turbines through Crusoe, with 13 additional ProEnergy units ordered in April 2026 . This document acknowledges that BTM generation has physically accelerated time-to-power for these specific deployments. The physical deadlock addressed here is not whether BTM works at the margin. It is whether BTM scales as a general topology beyond the finite inventory of available generation assets.

❌ **Core Physical Deadlock**: BTM generation is physically constrained by the supply chain for hot-section turbine components—turbine blades and vanes manufactured from nickel-based superalloys via directional solidification or single-crystal casting. The global qualified casting capacity for industrial gas turbine hot-section components is concentrated in three foundries: PCC (Precision Castparts Corp), Howmet Aerospace, and Doncasters. Order books for new industrial gas turbines from GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, and Mitsubishi Power extend beyond 2030 . The casting lead time is a metallurgical and capacity constraint, not a permitting constraint. BTM does not bypass the interconnect queue; it substitutes a metallurgical queue with a longer time constant for an electrical queue with a shorter one.

❌ **Engineering Misconception**: The assumption that BTM generation is a scalable general solution is physically incomplete. The current BTM deployments at Colossus 2 and Stargate rely on two finite inventories: (1) portable or trailer-mounted turbines originally manufactured for peaking or temporary service, and (2) aeroderivative units retrofitted from retired commercial aircraft engine cores (GE CF6-80C2, rated ~50 MW per unit) . The retired engine inventory is physically finite and geographically dispersed. ProEnergy’s PE6000 production line converts existing cores; it does not manufacture new hot-section castings at scale. Once the available core inventory is exhausted, BTM capacity expansion reverts to the same casting queue that constrains new-build industrial turbines.

✅ **System-Level Compensation Topology (Only Exit)**: The only physical exit from the casting queue deadlock is to decouple compute deployment from new-build turbine supply entirely. Three paths exist: (A) Aeroderivative retrofit using the finite retired engine inventory—this is a bridge, not a solution, with a physical terminus defined by the number of serviceable CF6-class cores in existence; (B) Acquisition of existing grid-connected generation capacity—a zero-sum transfer that does not create new electrons, only redistributes them; (C) Orbital compute offload, where photovoltaic area and radiative area are the only constraints, and the turbine casting supply chain is physically absent from the critical path.

**Objective Strategic Impact**
· For Tier-1 Giants: The BTM window is physically finite. Companies that secured aeroderivative retrofit capacity or existing generation assets before the inventory depletion point hold a temporary advantage measured in months, not years. The casting queue for new-build industrial turbines remains the binding constraint beyond that window. Capital deployed into new-build BTM projects without secured casting allocations enters a queue with a delivery date beyond the GPU depreciation horizon.
· For Tier-2 Enterprises: The BTM path is not a general escape from the interconnect queue. It is a finite-inventory arbitrage available to early movers. Enterprises entering the BTM queue after the aeroderivative inventory is allocated face the same casting constraint as the grid interconnect queue, with the added cost of turbine procurement premiums.

**02. 表后发电绕行队列：涡轮热端铸件的全球产能死锁**

xAI已在孟菲斯Colossus 2站点部署表后发电（BTM），绕过了田纳西河谷管理局的电网互联队列。截至2026年11月，xAI在密西西比州Southaven的前杜克能源厂址安装了约57台便携式燃气轮机，该厂址位于州界对面、距孟菲斯仅数英里，在永久性PSD许可下容量正向1.2 GW扩展。OpenAI在得克萨斯州阿比林的Stargate站点同样通过Crusoe部署了燃气轮机，并于2026年4月追加订购13台ProEnergy机组。本文承认BTM发电已在物理上加速了上述具体部署的通电时间。此处指出的物理死锁不是BTM在边际上是否有效。它是BTM作为通用拓扑，能否超越可用发电资产的有限库存而规模化。

❌ **核心物理死锁**：BTM发电在物理上受限于涡轮热端部件——涡轮叶片与导向叶片，由镍基高温合金经定向凝固或单晶铸造工艺制造——的供应链。工业燃气轮机热端部件的全球合格铸造产能集中于三家铸造厂：PCC（Precision Castparts Corp）、Howmet Aerospace与Doncasters。GE Vernova、西门子能源与三菱重工的新建工业燃气轮机订单簿已排至2030年之后。铸造交付周期是冶金与产能约束，不是许可约束。BTM没有绕过互联队列；它用一个时间常数更长的冶金队列，替换了一个时间常数更短的电气队列。

❌ **工程认知误区**：假设BTM发电是可规模化的通用解决方案，在物理上不完整。当前Colossus 2与Stargate的BTM部署依赖两类有限库存：（1）原为调峰或临时服务制造的便携式或拖车式涡轮机；（2）从退役商用飞机发动机核心机（GE CF6-80C2，额定约50 MW/台）翻新的航改机组。退役发动机库存物理上有限且地理分散。ProEnergy的PE6000产线改造现有核心机；它不以规模制造新的热端铸件。一旦可用核心机库存耗尽，BTM容量扩展回归到约束新建工业涡轮机的同一铸造队列。

✅ **系统级代偿拓扑（唯一生路）**：铸造队列死锁的唯一物理生路，是将算力部署与新建涡轮机供应彻底解耦。三条路径存在：（A）使用有限退役发动机库存的航改机组翻新——这是桥梁，不是解决方案，其物理终点由现存的CF6级可维修核心机数量定义；（B）收购现有并网发电容量——零和转移，不创造新电子，只重新分配；（C）轨道算力卸载，其中光伏面积与辐射面积是唯一约束，涡轮铸造供应链从关键路径中物理缺席。

**客观战略影响**
· 对一线巨头：BTM窗口在物理上有限。在库存耗尽点之前锁定航改机组翻新容量或现有发电资产的企业，持有以月而非年计量的临时优势。超出该窗口后，新建工业涡轮机的铸造队列仍是约束。投入新建BTM项目而未锁定铸件配额的资本，进入的队列交付日期超出GPU折旧视界。
· 对二线企业：BTM路径不是逃离互联队列的通用出口。它是早期行动者可用的有限库存套利。在航改机组库存分配完毕后进入BTM队列的企业，面临与电网互联队列相同的铸造约束，外加涡轮采购溢价的成本。
