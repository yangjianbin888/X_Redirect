An extreme interplanetary CME can drive Dst to approximately −2500 nT, the magnetospheric saturation threshold beyond which additional solar wind energy does not further intensify the ring current but instead couples into other channels, including the ionosphere and the ground . At this saturation limit, the magnetopause standoff distance compresses to approximately 4 Earth radii from the surface, exposing orbiting assets to direct solar particle radiation . The deposited energy manifests as geomagnetically induced currents (GICs) that enter power grid grounding points and flow through transformer windings as quasi-DC currents .

GIC magnitudes exceeding 100 A per transformer cause half-cycle saturation of magnetic cores, producing hotspot formation, reactive power loss spikes, and harmonic generation . A 1-in-10,000-year storm—above the Carrington benchmark—would result in approximately 1% of the population in Europe and North America experiencing long-term (months to years) electricity loss, even assuming a uniform 33% high-voltage transformer spare capacity . The physical constraint is not protection device availability; it is the thermal time constant of transformer insulation versus the duration of the GIC exposure.
