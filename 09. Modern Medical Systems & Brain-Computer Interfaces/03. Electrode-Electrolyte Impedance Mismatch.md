**❌ Core Physical Deadlock:** The electrode-electrolyte interface is not a resistor. It is a double-layer capacitor in series with a Faradaic charge-transfer resistance. At the recording frequency band, the interfacial impedance magnitude is bounded by the double-layer capacitance per unit electrochemically active area. For a metal electrode with geometric area A_geo = 100 μm², the double-layer capacitance C_dl falls in the picofarad range under physiological ionic strength. Derivation: C_dl = ε_r·ε_0·A_eff/d, with ε_r ≈ 80 (water), ε_0 = 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m, A_eff ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m², d ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m (Debye length scale): C_dl ≈ 7 pF. At 1 kHz, the capacitive reactance is 1/(2π·10³·7×10⁻¹²) ≈ 22.7 MΩ. This is the physical floor for a bare metal electrode of this geometry. Surface roughening increases A_eff only up to the pore depth where ionic diffusion at the frequency of interest remains accessible.

**❌ Engineering Misconception:** “Lower 1 kHz impedance = solved charge injection.” The 1 kHz impedance is a small-signal AC parameter. Recording fidelity is governed by the thermal noise floor: V_n = sqrt(4k_B·T·R·Δf), where R is the real part of the interfacial impedance. For R = 1 MΩ and Δf = 10 kHz: V_n ≈ 4.1 μV_rms. Extracellular spike amplitudes are 50–500 μV_pp. SNR is bounded by the interface, not the amplifier. Critically, in vivo data shows that reducing impedance below ~250 kΩ does not consistently improve spike sorting performance, because biological noise dominates over thermal noise in the living brain . The impedance ceiling is not the only constraint; the noise floor is co-determined by biology.
