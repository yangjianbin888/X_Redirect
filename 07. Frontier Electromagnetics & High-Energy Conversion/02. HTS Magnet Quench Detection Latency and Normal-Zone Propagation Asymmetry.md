### 02. HTS Magnet Quench Detection Latency and Normal-Zone Propagation Asymmetry — A Detection-Latency Redline

**Part 1: Current State** (as of 2026-11)

The fusion magnet community has demonstrated HTS coils operating at 20 K and above with current densities exceeding 200 A/mm², and the first generation of HTS tokamak magnets has entered construction. REBCO coated conductors are now available in kilometer-class lengths with consistent critical current. These are legitimate engineering achievements. The following analysis is not a dismissal of that work. It is a protection-physics boundary check: what detection latency is physically admissible when the normal zone in an HTS conductor does not behave like its LTS counterpart.

❌ **Core Physical Deadlock**

Low-temperature superconductors (LTS, e.g., NbTi, Nb₃Sn) exhibit normal-zone propagation velocities in the range of 1–10 m/s. A quench initiated at a single point in an LTS coil produces a voltage signature that rises measurably within milliseconds; voltage-based detection has time to act before the hot spot reaches damaging temperature.

HTS coated conductors do not behave this way. Their normal-zone propagation velocity is one to two orders of magnitude lower — on the order of 0.01–0.1 m/s — because the stabilizer-to-superconductor area ratio and the thermal conductivity anisotropy of the REBCO architecture suppress longitudinal heat transport. The local hot-spot temperature can therefore rise to the point of insulation degradation or substrate damage while the terminal voltage across the coil remains below the noise floor of conventional detection electronics.

The absolute protection redline is:

t_detect ≥ t_damage

where t_detect is the time from quench initiation to a detectable signal, and t_damage is the time for the local hot spot to reach the insulation degradation threshold. For HTS conductors under representative operating conditions, t_damage can be on the order of tens to hundreds of milliseconds. If the normal zone propagates at 0.01 m/s, a hot spot 1 mm in extent requires 100 ms to grow to 2 mm. The voltage generated by that 1 mm normal zone, given the low electric field in the normal state at these current densities, may be below 1 mV across a coil with tens of meters of conductor. Conventional voltage detection with a noise floor of 10–100 μV cannot resolve this signal before the hot spot has already done its damage.

❌ **Engineering Cognitive Trap**

The standard response is: “increase sampling rate” or “lower the noise floor.” Both fail against the same wall.

Faster electronics do not create a voltage signal that the physics has not yet generated. If the normal-zone length is 1 mm and the local electric field is 1 V/m, the voltage is 1 mV. Lowering the noise floor to 1 μV improves the signal-to-noise ratio, but the absolute voltage remains 1 mV — and at the moment that voltage becomes measurable, the hot-spot temperature may already have crossed the degradation threshold. The detection problem is not one of electronic sensitivity. It is one of physical signal latency: the HTS conductor's thermal and electrical properties conspire to delay the voltage signature until it is too late.

✅ **System-Level Compensation Topology**

The only physically admissible path is to detect the quench before the voltage signature appears, using a signal generated by the thermal event itself rather than by the normal-zone resistance.

**Path A: Distributed Fiber-Optic Acoustic/Temperature Sensing**

Optical fibers embedded within or bonded to the HTS coil can detect the local temperature rise or the acoustic emission associated with the onset of a normal zone. The physical constraint is spatial resolution: the sensing fiber must resolve a hot spot smaller than the minimum propagation length scale. For a hot spot of 1 mm and a fiber with 1 mm spatial resolution, the detection latency is set by the speed of light in fiber (≈ 2 × 10⁸ m/s) plus the signal processing time, which can be below 1 μs. The thermal constraint is that the fiber must be in intimate thermal contact with the conductor, which imposes a co-winding or co-lamination requirement on the coil architecture.

**Path B: Current Redistribution Detection via Hall Probe Array**

When a local normal zone forms in an HTS conductor, the transport current redistributes to the surrounding stabilizer and to adjacent turns. A Hall probe array positioned at the coil ends or at strategic locations can detect this redistribution as a change in the local magnetic field. The physical constraint is sensitivity: the probe must resolve a current redistribution of less than 0.1% of the transport current. For a 10 kA conductor, this means resolving 10 A, which is within the capability of commercial Hall probes but requires careful magnetic shielding from the main coil field.

**Objective Strategic Impact**

· **For Tier-1 Giants:** The current approach of scaling HTS coil size and field strength has a hard protection ceiling. Larger coils store more energy, and the tolerable detection latency scales inversely with stored energy: a 1 GJ coil with a 100 ms detection latency is already at the edge of protection feasibility. The moment of recognition arrives when a full-scale coil quenches without a detectable voltage signature, and the protection system fires after the hot spot has already degraded the insulation. The physical lag to re-validate a fiber-optic or Hall-probe topology is approximately 12–18 months from the decision point.

· **For Tier-2 Enterprises:** Distributed fiber sensing does not require a new conductor architecture. It requires a co-winding or co-lamination capability and a signal-processing capability. The physical window to establish a validated detection-latency measurement on a representative HTS coil segment is approximately 6–9 months.

The normal-zone propagation velocity of 0.01–0.1 m/s is a material property of the HTS conductor, not a parameter that improves with electronics. From the 2026-11 anchor: the coil you qualify this year with voltage-based detection has a detection margin that shrinks as coil size grows. A 100 m conductor length at 0.01 m/s propagation means a quench initiated at one end takes 10,000 seconds to traverse the coil — but the hot spot at the initiation point reaches damage temperature in milliseconds. By the time the voltage-based system detects the normal zone, the insulation at the initiation point has already sustained irreversible degradation. Your protection system is not protecting the coil. It is recording the aftermath.

The physical loss is the coil plus the diagnostic data from the failed test. The time lost is 12–18 months of re-design without a validated detection model.

It fails because the voltage signature is generated too slowly by the HTS normal zone, independent of current level. A lower current reduces the hot-spot heating rate but does not change the propagation velocity. The detection latency gap remains.

### 02. 高温超导磁体失超检测延迟与常态区传播不对称——检测延迟红线

**第一部分：现状**（截至 2026 年 11 月）

聚变磁体领域已在 20 K 及以上温区实现 HTS 线圈电流密度超过 200 A/mm²，第一代 HTS 托卡马克磁体已进入建造阶段。REBCO 涂层导体已可提供公里级长度且临界电流一致。这些是真实的工程成就。以下分析不是对这些工作的否定。它是对保护物理的边界校验：当 HTS 导体中的常态区行为与 LTS 对应物不同时，多大的检测延迟在物理上是可允许的。

❌ **核心物理死锁**

低温超导体（LTS，如 NbTi、Nb₃Sn）的常态区传播速度在 1–10 m/s 量级。LTS 线圈中单点起始的失超产生的电压信号在毫秒内可测量上升，电压检测有足够时间在热点达到损伤温度前动作。

HTS 涂层导体不这样行为。其常态区传播速度低一到两个数量级——在 0.01–0.1 m/s 量级——因为稳定层与超导层的面积比以及 REBCO 架构的热导率各向异性抑制了纵向热输运。因此局部热点温度可以上升到绝缘退化或基板损伤的程度，而线圈端电压仍低于常规检测电子设备的噪声底。

绝对保护红线是：

t_detect ≥ t_damage

其中 t_detect 为失超起始到可检测信号的时间，t_damage 为局部热点达到绝缘退化阈值的时间。对于代表性工况下的 HTS 导体，t_damage 可在数十到数百毫秒量级。如果常态区以 0.01 m/s 传播，1 mm 范围的热点需要 100 ms 增长到 2 mm。该 1 mm 常态区产生的电压，鉴于这些电流密度下常态的低电场，在数十米导体的线圈上可能低于 1 mV。噪声底为 10–100 μV 的常规电压检测无法在热点已经造成损伤之前分辨该信号。

❌ **工程认知误区**

标准回应是：“提高采样率”或“降低噪声底”。两者撞的是同一堵墙。

更快的电子设备不会创造一个物理尚未产生的电压信号。如果常态区长度为 1 mm，局部电场为 1 V/m，电压就是 1 mV。将噪声底降至 1 μV 改善信噪比，但绝对电压仍是 1 mV——而当该电压变得可测量的那一刻，热点温度可能已经越过退化阈值。检测问题不是电子灵敏度的问题。它是物理信号延迟的问题：HTS 导体的热和电属性合谋将电压信号延迟到为时已晚。

✅ **系统级代偿拓扑**

唯一物理上可允许的路径是在电压信号出现之前检测失超，使用由热事件本身产生的信号而非常态区电阻产生的信号。

**路径 A：分布式光纤声学/温度传感**

嵌入或粘合到 HTS 线圈中的光纤可以检测与常态区起始相关的局部温升或声发射。物理约束是空间分辨率：传感光纤必须分辨小于最小传播长度尺度的热点。对于 1 mm 热点和 1 mm 空间分辨率的光纤，检测延迟由光纤中的光速（≈ 2 × 10⁸ m/s）加信号处理时间决定，可低于 1 μs。热约束是光纤必须与导体紧密热接触，这在线圈架构上施加了共绕或共层压要求。

**路径 B：霍尔探头阵列电流再分布检测**

当 HTS 导体中形成局部常态区时，传输电流再分布到周围稳定层和相邻匝。位于线圈端部或战略位置的霍尔探头阵列可以将这种再分布检测为局部磁场的变化。物理约束是灵敏度：探头必须分辨小于传输电流 0.1% 的电流再分布。对于 10 kA 导体，这意味着分辨 10 A，在商用霍尔探头能力范围内，但需要仔细的主线圈磁场屏蔽。

**客观战略影响**

· **对一线巨头：** 当前扩展 HTS 线圈尺寸和场强的路径存在硬保护天花板。更大的线圈储存更多能量，可容忍的检测延迟与储存能量成反比：1 GJ 线圈在 100 ms 检测延迟下已处于保护可行性边缘。认知时刻将在全尺寸线圈失超且无可检测电压前兆、保护系统在热点已退化绝缘之后才动作时到来。重新验证光纤或霍尔探头拓扑的物理滞后约为 12–18 个月，从决策点起算。

· **对二线企业：** 分布式光纤传感不需要新的导体架构。它需要共绕或共层压能力以及信号处理能力。在代表性 HTS 线圈段上建立经验证检测延迟测量的物理窗口约为 6–9 个月。

⚠️ **爆雷倒计时 —— 锚定 2026 年 11 月**

0.01–0.1 m/s 的常态区传播速度是 HTS 导体的材料属性，不是随电子设备改善的参数。自 2026 年 11 月锚点起算：你们今年用电压检测鉴定的线圈，其检测裕度随线圈尺寸增长而收缩。100 m 导体长度在 0.01 m/s 传播下意味着单端起始的失超需要 10,000 秒穿越线圈——但起始点的热点在毫秒内达到损伤温度。当电压检测系统检测到常态区时，起始点的绝缘已经承受了不可逆退化。你们的保护系统不是在保护线圈。它是在记录后果。

**物理终局清算**

· ❌ **不买：** 项目继续电压检测加更快电子设备。每次迭代产生增量噪声底改善，直到全尺寸线圈失超发生且无可检测电压前兆。物理损失是线圈加上失败测试的诊断数据。时间损失是 12–18 个月的无验证检测模型重新设计。
